I am a molecular, evolutionary, and computational biologist with extensive
experience in macroevolution, population genomics, bioinformatics, and
statistical analysis. My research integrates large-scale genomic datasets,
computational tool development, and evolutionary approaches to investigate the
genomic mechanisms underlying adaptation in natural populations.

I am currently a **Genomicist–Bioinformatician Researcher at the Barcelona
Institute for Global Health (ISGlobal)**, where I work on the *GenMoz* project,
analysing high-resolution genomic data from malaria parasites to assess genomic
metrics associated with epidemic dynamics and transmission intensity over time
in children and pregnant women (PI: Alfredo Mayor).

I am also an **Associate / Visiting Researcher in the
[Nachman Lab](https://www.nachmanlab.org)** at the Museum of Vertebrate Zoology,
University of California, Berkeley, where I study how transposable-element
polymorphisms and structural variation contribute to adaptation in wild house
mouse populations.

Research interests
======
My research focuses on understanding the genomic mechanisms underlying species
adaptation to extremes (environments or physiology) through the analysis of
large-scale genomic datasets and the development of bioinformatics tools. My
work includes:

1. studying the genomic basis of **environmental adaptation in mammals** using
   genome–environment association approaches;
2. exploring the role of **transposable-element polymorphisms** in driving
   mechanisms of environmental adaptation;
3. **genomic surveillance for public health** — malaria genomics to support
   elimination efforts;
4. investigating the genomic basis of **dietary diversification and
   specialization** in animals.
